# Walk Mode

← [[ninety-nine/src/Source Code|Source Code]]

The third option beside every scene's two choices from S1 on: **WALK THE ROOM**. Unscored -- it never touches conformance, dissonance, friction or flags -- and it leaves the drone, bed and music running (C7). Built the way the museum walkthrough in the Earth_Worldbuild repo is built: no GLB, no Blender export; a JSON layout per room, procedural box geometry, world-tiled textures, first-person controls with axis-separated raycast collision. Then the game's own look on top, and canon C1 as a material rule: the building is textured (512 px colour + bump + roughness, trilinear/anisotropic), lit, shadowed (soft 2048 px shadow map) and finished by a composite pass (ACES, vignette, film grain, dither) so it reads like the photoreal plates; everything the company owns is flat-shaded low-poly with PS1 vertex snapping and no texture at all. The first cut rendered the whole frame at a third of the viewport with nearest-neighbour upscaling and 256 px filterless textures, which read as "low quality" rather than as a style -- the PS1 treatment now stays on the props, where the design puts it.

Canon, enforced in code: **C1** two-tier materials ([[ninety-nine/src/walk/materials.js|materials.js]]); **C2** the one shadow-casting light sits on the entrance side and aims at the core, so every shadow falls toward the core ([[ninety-nine/src/walk/room.js|room.js]]); **C3** rooms are closed on every side unless `open` names one, and then fog or glass closes it (asserted by `rooms.validate.js`); **C5**/**C6** figures are static boxes with low-poly hands; **C8** exactly one handless clock per built room (asserted).

- **[[ninety-nine/src/walk/launcher.js|launcher.js]]** — what router.js talks to. Looks up `S{n}_{C|H}` in `data/rooms.json`, dynamic-imports the rest on the first click (three.js is 1.3 MB; the cold start stays at Doc 4 §8.2's 2 s), pauses the video layer, toggles `body.walking` (which is what stops Escape from reading as the fire exit while the pointer is locked).
- **[[ninety-nine/src/walk/walk.js|walk.js]]** — renderer, camera, scene, loop, HUD (LEAVE THE ROOM + hint), footsteps via sfx.js's `footstep` cue, per-room surface-tinted, head-bob on the same stride. Renders into an MSAA half-float target at 70% of the viewport and composites it with a small full-screen shader (ACES tone map, vignette, grain, dither; per-room `exposure` / `grain` / `vignette` from the record).
- **[[ninety-nine/src/walk/room.js|room.js]]** — builds a room from a record: floor/ceiling/walls from `size`, `skirt` / `cornice` bands on every closed wall, `beams` (ceiling downstands, skipped across an `open` side), extra `boxes` (pillars, water, ledges, barriers), `props`, lights (`flicker` on a light = a per-fixture sodium/fluorescent buzz, never in unison), fog, spawn.
- **[[ninety-nine/src/walk/props.js|props.js]]** — the company's things: chair, desk, monitor, partition, door, glass doors, curtain, sign, badge reader, counter, dispenser, figure, car (hood, trunk, glass band, bumpers, 8-sided wheels, lit tail lights, an open door on the `hero`), elevator panel, shelf, cooler, box tower, package, mop sink, drain, pillar (plinth, cap, painted `band`), fluoro and sodium fixtures with housings and additive glow plates, pendant, wall plate, pipe run, emblem, placard, slip, clock. Mirrors the archetypes in [[blender/prop_builders.py|blender/prop_builders.py]].
- **[[ninety-nine/src/walk/materials.js|materials.js]]** — canvas-drawn procedural textures (marble, plaster, concrete, painted concrete, carpet, cinderblock, linoleum, ceiling tile, pool tile, water, cardboard, rust, grate, glass…): each slot draws a colour map, a height map (grout, seams, cracks recessed) and a roughness map (wet and worn spots glossy), tiled in world units; the water drifts per frame. The `lp_*` flat palette with the vertex-snap shader patch, plus `glow_*` additive fixture glows.
- **[[ninety-nine/src/walk/controls.js|controls.js]]** — WASD / arrows, pointer-lock or drag look, touch joystick + drag-look; ported from the museum's controls.js and touch.js.

Data and tools: [[ninety-nine/data/rooms.json|data/rooms.json]] is generated by [[ninety-nine/tools/build_rooms.py|tools/build_rooms.py]] (edit that, not the JSON). Preview any room without playing to it at `/test/rooms.html?room=S4_C` ([[ninety-nine/test/Tests|test/]]). three.js r160 is vendored at `ninety-nine/vendor/three/` (MIT, licence alongside).
